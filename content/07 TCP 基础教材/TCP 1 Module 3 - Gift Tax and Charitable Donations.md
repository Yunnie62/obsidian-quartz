# TCP 1 Module 3 - Gift Tax and Charitable Donations

## Module Purpose

This module explains gift taxation, the unified transfer tax system, taxable gifts, annual and unlimited exclusions, present vs. future interests, complete vs. incomplete gifts, recipient basis, and charitable contribution planning for noncash property.

## H - Highlight

### Key Numbers

- Unified credit base for 2023: cumulative taxable transfers of $12,920,000.
- Annual gift tax exclusion for 2023: $17,000 per donee.
- Gift splitting can create a $34,000 annual exclusion per donee.
- Cash charitable contributions to public charities: generally subject to a 60% of AGI limitation.
- Ordinary income property contributions: generally subject to a 50% of AGI limitation.
- LTCG property contributions: generally subject to a 30% of AGI limitation.
- Excess charitable contributions may be carried forward for five years.
- Noncash contributions over $500 require Form 8283.
- Noncash contributions over $5,000 generally require a qualified appraisal.

## A - Ask

- Is the transfer a taxable gift?
- Is the gift a present interest or future interest?
- Is the gift complete or incomplete?
- Does an annual exclusion or unlimited exclusion apply?
- What is the recipient's basis?
- Is charitable property ordinary income property or long-term capital gain property?
- Is tangible personal property used for a related or unrelated charitable purpose?
- Which AGI limitation applies?
- Is substantiation required?

## R - Rephrase

1. A gift tax issue starts with whether the transfer is for less than adequate consideration.
2. The annual exclusion generally requires a present interest.
3. Only complete gifts are subject to gift tax and eligible for the annual exclusion.
4. The recipient generally does not recognize income from receiving a gift.
5. Charitable deductions for noncash property depend on property type, holding period, appreciation, and charitable use.

## S - Synthesize

### High-Frequency Rules

- The annual exclusion applies per donee, per year.
- Future interest gifts generally do not qualify for the annual exclusion.
- Transfers between spouses may qualify for the unlimited marital deduction.
- Ordinary income property deductions are limited to the lesser of adjusted basis or FMV.
- LTCG property deductions are generally based on FMV, unless a special limitation applies.
- Tangible personal property used for an unrelated purpose can reduce the deduction to adjusted basis.

### Common Traps

- Applying the annual exclusion to a future interest gift.
- Forgetting gift splitting.
- Treating every noncash charitable donation as deductible at FMV.
- Ignoring related-use rules for tangible personal property.
- Forgetting Form 8283 or appraisal requirements for larger noncash contributions.
