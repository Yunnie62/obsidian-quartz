# June 2026 Updates Applied Summary

## Purpose

This note converts the June 2026 course update pages into study-ready update points. Use this page when reviewing TCP 1 and TCP 2 topics that changed in version 1.4.

## TCP 1 Module 1 Updates

### 2026 Standard Deduction

- Single: $16,100
- Head of household: $24,150
- Married filing jointly or surviving spouse: $32,200
- Married filing separately: $16,100

### Additional Standard Deduction for Age 65 or Older and/or Blindness

- Unmarried, one qualified status: $2,050
- Unmarried, both 65 and blind: $4,100
- Married, one qualified taxpayer: $1,650
- Married, both 65 and blind for one qualified taxpayer: $3,300
- Married, two qualified taxpayers each 65 or blind: $3,300
- Married, two qualified taxpayers both 65 and blind: $6,600

### Charitable Deduction if Standard Deduction Taken

Individual taxpayers who do not itemize can deduct cash contributions to public charities up to:

- $1,000
- $2,000 for MFJ

### Itemized Deduction Updates

- Medical expenses: deductible in excess of 7.5% of AGI.
- State and local taxes: limited to $40,400 in 2026.
- Charitable contributions: subject to a 0.5% of AGI floor.
- Casualty/theft losses: limited to federal or state-declared disasters, with per-casualty and AGI limitations.

## TCP 1 Module 4 Updates

### RMDs From Traditional 401(k)s

RMDs from traditional 401(k)s must begin by April 1 of the year after the later of:

- the year the employee reaches age 73, if born between 1951 and 1959;
- the year the employee reaches age 75, if born in 1960 or later; or
- the year the employee terminates employment with the plan sponsor.

Roth 401(k) plans are not subject to these RMD requirements.

Failure-to-take-RMD penalty:

- 25%
- reduced to 10% if corrected in a timely manner

### 2026 IRA Limits

- Under age 50: $7,500
- Age 50 or older: $8,600
- Married under age 50: $15,000 total
- Married age 50 or older: $17,200 total

### 2026 Traditional IRA Deduction Phase-Out

If taxpayer participates in an employer-sponsored retirement plan:

- Unmarried: $81,000-$91,000
- MFJ: $129,000-$149,000

Special spousal rule if taxpayer is not an active participant but spouse is:

- MFJ: $242,000-$252,000
- MFS: $0-$10,000

### 2026 Roth IRA Contribution Phase-Out

- Unmarried: $153,000-$168,000
- MFJ: $242,000-$252,000
- MFS: $0-$10,000

### Trump Accounts

Trump accounts are tax-deferred investment accounts for children under age 18 with a valid Social Security number.

- Starting July 4, 2026, eligible children under 18 may open and contribute.
- U.S. citizen children born between January 1, 2025 and December 31, 2028, with a Social Security number, receive a one-time $1,000 federal contribution under the pilot program.
- Employers may contribute up to $2,500 per year for an employee or dependent.
- Maximum total contribution: $5,000 per year.
- No deduction is allowed for contributions.
- Employer and federal pilot contributions are excluded from income.
- No distributions before the calendar year the beneficiary turns 18.
- At age 18 or older, regular traditional IRA distribution rules apply.

## TCP 2 Updates

### Section 1244 Small Business Stock

Maximum ordinary loss deduction:

- MFJ: $100,000
- Other individual filers: $50,000

### Section 1202 QSBS

Aggregate gross assets limitation:

- Stock issued on or before July 4, 2025: $50 million
- Stock issued after July 4, 2025: $75 million

Maximum exclusion per shareholder:

- Stock acquired on or before July 4, 2025: greater of 10 times basis or $10 million ($5 million if MFS)
- Stock acquired after July 4, 2025: greater of 10 times basis or $15 million ($7.5 million if MFS) for 2026

If the exclusion percentage is less than 100%, the 28% capital gain rate applies to the nonexcluded portion of the QSBS gain within the maximum exclusion amount.

### Consolidated Taxable Income Calculation

Updated consolidated taxable income steps:

1. Calculate each member's stand-alone taxable income.
2. Remove effects of intercompany transactions.
3. Remove items determined at the consolidated level.
4. Combine member taxable incomes.
5. Apply consolidated-level items.

Consolidated-level items include capital gains/losses, Section 1231 gains/losses, NOL, charitable contribution deduction, and DRD.
