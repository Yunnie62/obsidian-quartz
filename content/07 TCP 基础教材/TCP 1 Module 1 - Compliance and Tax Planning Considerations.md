# TCP 1 Module 1 - Compliance and Tax Planning Considerations

## Module Purpose

This module explains how individual taxpayers recognize income, plan deductions, manage timing decisions, and avoid penalties. It also introduces several high-frequency TCP topics: equity compensation, below-market loans, foreign-earned income exclusion, AMT, kiddie tax, FSA/HSA rules, standard vs. itemized deductions, estimated tax payments, and year-end planning.

## H - Highlight

### Rules and Thresholds

- Equity compensation tax timing depends on the award type and key dates.
- Below-market loans may create imputed interest.
- Foreign-earned income may be excluded if the taxpayer satisfies a qualification test.
- Timing decisions should compare after-tax results using present value.
- AMTI starts with regular taxable income and then applies AMT adjustments and preference items.
- Kiddie tax applies to net unearned income of certain dependent children.
- FSAs generally have a use-it-or-lose-it feature; HSAs can accumulate.
- Taxpayers compare standard deduction with itemized deductions.
- Estimated tax penalties can be avoided through safe harbors.

### Key Numbers From This Module

- Foreign-earned income exclusion: up to $120,000 for 2023.
- Physical presence test: 330 full days in a foreign country during any 12-consecutive-month period.
- ISO holding period: at least 2 years after grant date and at least 1 year after exercise date.
- ISO annual exercise limit: $100,000.
- De minimis loan exception: $10,000 or less, subject to limitations.
- Gift loans of $100,000 or less: special limitation based on net investment income.
- HSA 2023 contribution limit: $3,850 individual; $7,750 family; additional $1,000 for age 55 or older.
- 2023 standard deduction: $13,850 single; $20,800 head of household; $27,700 married filing jointly.
- Estimated tax safe harbor: less than $1,000 owed, or pay the lesser of 90% of current-year tax or 100% of prior-year tax; 110% if prior-year AGI exceeds $150,000.
- Failure-to-pay penalty: generally 0.5% per month, up to 25%.

## A - Ask

- What type of compensation or transaction is being tested?
- What is the taxable event?
- Is the income ordinary income, capital gain, or excluded?
- Does a threshold, phase-out, holding period, or exception apply?
- Is the question asking for tax liability, taxable income, basis, or planning advice?
- Would accelerating or deferring income/deductions improve the after-tax result?

## R - Rephrase

1. For equity compensation, identify the award type before calculating income.
2. For below-market loans, the tax law may treat foregone interest as if it had been paid.
3. For foreign-earned income, qualification comes before the exclusion amount.
4. For timing decisions, compare the present value of tax paid or saved.
5. For AMT, start with regular taxable income and adjust to AMTI.
6. For deduction planning, standard deduction is the baseline; itemizing only helps when qualified itemized deductions exceed it.
7. For estimated tax, safe harbor rules matter as much as the final tax due.

## S - Synthesize

### High-Frequency Rules

- Equity compensation questions are usually tax timing questions.
- Below-market loans convert a missing interest payment into deemed tax consequences.
- Foreign-earned income exclusion requires either the bona fide residence test or physical presence test.
- AMT adjusts regular taxable income for items treated differently under AMT rules.
- Kiddie tax shifts part of a child's unearned income to the parents' tax rate.
- HSA is more flexible than FSA because unused HSA funds can accumulate.
- Bunching itemized deductions can create a benefit when annual itemized deductions are close to the standard deduction.

### Common Traps

- Treating all stock-related compensation as capital gain.
- Forgetting that ISO exercise can create an AMT preference item.
- Applying the foreign-earned income exclusion before checking qualification.
- Comparing future tax amounts without discounting to present value.
- Assuming itemized deductions always help.
- Using the 100% prior-year estimated tax safe harbor when the 110% rule applies.
